Campbell Cannabis Delivery: What the 2025 Ordinance Changed

For years, Campbell maintained municipal code provisions that restricted commercial cannabis activities within city limits. However, on April 1, 2025, the Campbell City Council formally adopted Ordinance 2327, amending the city's municipal code to allow lawful commercial delivery directly to adult residents. This guide explains what the ordinance changed, what remains restricted, and how Campbell residents can access legal cannabis.

What Ordinance 2327 Actually Changed

  • Repeal of Chapter 8.38: Deleted the city's historical blanket prohibition against cannabis deliveries.
  • Amendment of Chapter 8.40: Updated local standards to permit licensed cannabis retailers to deliver lawful cannabis products directly to adults aged 21 and older (or 18+ with medical documentation) at private residences.
  • Alignment with State Law: Harmonized Campbell’s municipal regulations with California Department of Cannabis Control (DCC) delivery standards.

Downtown Campbell and The Pruneyard: Public Restrictions

Campbell is celebrated for its historic downtown dining strip along East Campbell Avenue and the Pruneyard shopping center:

  • Public Outings Are Smoke-Free: Proximity to a dispensary delivery radius does not alter public smoking bans. Consuming cannabis in public dining plazas, outdoor shopping corridors, or parking facilities is illegal under California state law.
  • Community Events: Popular community gatherings, such as the Campbell Farmers Market or Boogie Music Festival, are designated public events where cannabis consumption is strictly prohibited.
  • Private Residences Only: Deliveries must be received and consumed on private property outside public view.

Practical Advice for Campbell Residents

  1. Verify Your Delivery Service: Confirm that your dispensary is licensed by the California DCC and dispatches drivers with verified manifests.
  2. Prepare Photo ID: Keep your physical driver's license or government ID ready for verification at the door.
  3. Audit Your Receipt: Make sure your receipt reflects accurate California sales taxes and state excise charges.
