A Plain-Language Guide to Cannabis Categories and Labels

Cannabis packaging often displays a confusing mix of percentages, milligrams, batch codes, and marketing buzzwords. Learning to read past the branding gives you control over your experience, safety, and spending. This guide provides a straightforward method for reading labels and understanding product formats.

Focus on Format and Content, Not Mood Claims

Dispensaries carry a broad assortment of categories: flower, pre-rolls, extracts, vape cartridges, edibles, tinctures, and topicals. These categories describe physical delivery methods. Words like "energizing," "creative," or "deep sleep" are subjective marketing terms, not pharmacological guarantees.

Building a reliable label-reading habit means checking:

  1. Product format and net weight.
  2. Labeled cannabinoid concentrations (THC, CBD, minor cannabinoids).
  3. Serving sizes and total servings per package.
  4. Full ingredient lists and food allergen warnings.
  5. Batch and lot identifiers.

Percentages vs. Milligrams: Understanding the Units

  • Percentages (%): Represent the proportion of cannabinoids relative to total plant mass. A jar of flower labeled 20% THC contains roughly 200 milligrams of THC per gram of plant material.
  • Milligrams (mg): Represent the exact mass of active cannabinoids. Edibles, tinctures, and capsules are dosed in milligrams.
  • Why You Shouldn't Compare Them Directly: Inhaling flower delivers THC rapidly through the lungs into the bloodstream, whereas edibles are processed through the digestive tract and liver, creating 11-hydroxy-THC, a significantly more potent and longer-lasting compound. Comparing 20% flower directly to a 20 mg edible will lead to unexpected, uncomfortable experiences.

Reading a Fictional Edible Label Correctly

Consider a package of infused gummies labeled with a large "100 mg" badge on the front:

  • Total Package Content: 100 milligrams of total cannabinoids.
  • Serving Count: 10 equal servings.
  • Per-Serving Dose: 10 milligrams per serving (100 mg divided by 10).
  • Multi-Piece Servings: If the package contains 20 small gummies and 10 servings, one serving equals two gummies. Always verify the serving definition on the nutrition panel before consuming.

Understanding Ratios: THC, CBD, and Balance

Ratios express the proportional balance between active cannabinoids. For example, a 1:1 ratio contains equal amounts of two cannabinoids. However, order and total mass matter:

  • A 1:1 THC-to-CBD product with 5 mg of each delivers a mild dose.
  • A 1:1 THC-to-CBD product with 50 mg of each delivers a heavy dose.
  • Check whether the label reads "THC\:CBD" or "CBD\:THC" to confirm which compound is dominant.

Ingredient Panels and Allergen Safety

Cannabis edibles are prepared food products subject to food manufacturing standards. A label reading "natural strawberry" does not disclose whether the product uses gelatin (animal-derived), soy lecithin, coconut oil (tree nuts), or wheat flour.

Always read the certified ingredient panel. Similarly, topicals often contain essential oils, menthol, or nut-based carrier oils that can cause allergic reactions.

Extraction Terms: Live Resin, Rosin, and Distillate

  • Distillate: Highly refined cannabis oil that isolates specific cannabinoids (usually THC), stripping out natural terpenes and minor compounds.
  • Live Resin: Oil extracted from freshly frozen cannabis plants using hydrocarbon solvents, preserving natural terpenes and flavors.
  • Live Rosin: Solventless extract produced using only heat, pressure, and ice water.
  • While these terms describe production methods, they do not guarantee how an individual will feel.
